# The introduction (given before the first question)

Give it in your own words, briefly: two screens at most. The person must know what is coming, and why, before the interview begins. End by asking whether you should get started.

## What the person needs to know

**What is this?**
A starter kit that helps you get AI to work properly for you: better quality, a faster way of working, an overview at all times and fewer wasted tokens. It is not a ready-made package. It is built for you from what you tell me.

**Why does it exist?**
Most people who build with AI run into the same things: the AI forgets agreements from one session to the next, repeats the same mistakes, says "done" without having tried it, the folders get messy, and you do not know which model to choose. That is rarely because you are doing something wrong. It is because the foundation is missing. We build that now, once.

**It is a beta (say it early):**
The starter kit is a trial edition. You are among the first to use it, and your experience is what makes it better. Write "I'm stuck" or "I didn't understand that" at any time. Then I explain it in a different way, and I make a note of it. At the end I ask five short questions. Nothing is sent anywhere without you: you see all of it and decide yourself whether you want to send it.

**Short or full path (you choose):**
- Full path: everything below. Typically 1-2 hours in total, and you can spread it over several days. Gives rules and suggestions that fit exactly you.
- Short path: the computer is checked, the safety nets and the overview are set up, and the interview waits until later. Typically 20-40 minutes, and it uses far less of your usage.

**What will you go through? (the full path)**
1. A check that your computer has what is needed. If something is missing, I help you install it, one step at a time (5 minutes if everything is there; more if something has to be installed).
2. An interview about 8 topics (typically 30-60 minutes; you can take a break at any time). It is a conversation, not a form: there is no fixed number of questions. If you answer thoroughly, there will be fewer, and if you say something worth understanding better, I ask about it.
3. I look at what you already have, without changing anything.
4. I research the best solutions to exactly your problems and lay out a plan. You say yes or no to each suggestion.
5. We set up the foundation and try together that it works.
6. We build your overview: a page you open in your browser, so you can always see where you are.
7. We put a private backup of the project on GitHub, so the work is not only on one computer. You can opt out of that.

**Why so many questions?**
Because the result is exactly as good as your answers. A short answer gives a standard solution. A detailed answer gives a solution that fits you. I ask at most 2-3 things at a time, and I say each time why I am asking. If an answer is too thin for me to use, I ask once more with an example. "I don't know" is a perfectly good answer; then I note it as an open point.

Show the difference with an example:
- Thin: "I lack an overview."
- Good: "When I open the project after a week, I can't remember what works and what was half finished. Last time I spent a whole evening working out which folder was the newest."

**What do you get out of it?**
- Short, fixed rules that the AI reads at the start of every chat.
- A guard that stops the most dangerous mistakes (keys in the code, deleting work, skipping controls).
- A done-check, so that "done" means it has been tried.
- A status file and an overview, so you know where you are from one session to the next.
- A list of mistakes that must not happen again.
- Advice on which model to use for what, so you do not waste tokens.

**Getting through it costs tokens (say it before you start):**
Running the starter kit uses part of your usage: the interview is a long conversation, and the research afterwards reads sources on the web. See it as an investment. You spend tokens once on building the foundation, and afterwards you save them every day, because you do not have to explain the same thing from scratch, fix the same mistakes again or use an expensive model for a simple task. I cannot promise a number for how much you save.
Three things keep the cost down:
- Check your usage with `/usage` before we begin. If you are close to the limit, wait, or only do the interview today.
- The run can be spread over several days. Everything is saved, so nothing has to be repeated.
- The interview does not need the strongest model. We save that for the research and the plan, where it makes a difference.

**The honest caveat (say it clearly):**
The edition for Claude Code is new and is built from the edition for Codex. The individual parts are tested one by one, but the whole run has not been taken through Claude Code by a human. So there may be bugs. The English edition is also newer than the Danish one and has been tried by fewer people. If we hit a bug, I say so, find the cause and write it down, so it can be fixed in the kit.

**You decide.**
I change nothing without showing it first and getting your yes. You can stop at any time; everything is saved, and `/startkit` continues where we left off.

Ask at the end: "Shall we get started, and do you want to take the short or the full path?"
